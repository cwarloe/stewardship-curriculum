# Research Protocol

Adapted from `cwarloe/network-analyst-development-framework` (`docs/research/PROTOCOL.md`). This makes design claims traceable. It is applied research for a small seminar, not a systematic review.

## Workflow

1. **Frame the question.** Use [`template-research-question.md`](template-research-question.md): what decision it informs, and what would change the current view.
2. **Search reproducibly.** Record where you searched, the terms, and the date. Keep stable identifiers (DOI, journal, volume, pages).
3. **Record what was actually read.** Every source is one of:
   - **read**: the full text was consulted
   - **abstract**: the abstract or publisher summary only
   - **found**: located by search and summarized from secondary descriptions
   
   A "found" source is a lead, not evidence.
4. **Assess sources.** Record quality *and* applicability. A strong study of vocabulary recall is still indirect evidence for spiritual formation.
5. **Synthesize.** Group by claim, include conflicting findings, and state confidence and gaps. Sources aren't votes.
6. **Maintain.** Revisit claims when sources are read in full, superseded, or contradicted by what happens when the seminar actually runs.

If a research question is handed to an external model, keep the prompt verbatim in [`prompts/`](prompts/), named for the record it answers into.

## Confidence scale

- **High:** consistent, directly applicable evidence from multiple strong sources.
- **Moderate:** credible evidence with real limitations, indirectness, or some disagreement.
- **Low:** sparse, indirect, preliminary, or conflicting.
- **Unknown:** not yet assessed.

Confidence describes the evidence for a claim, not certainty that the seminar will work.

## The domain problem, stated once

Almost no controlled research exists on stewardship curricula, church small-group seminars, or retreat formats for adult faith formation. Nearly everything here is **borrowed across a domain boundary**: from learning science, habit research, financial education, workplace training transfer, and health behavior change. The borrowing is this project's claim, not the literature's. Label it every time.
